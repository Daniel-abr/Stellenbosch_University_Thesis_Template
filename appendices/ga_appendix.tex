\section{Complex Engineering Problem}
\label{app:complex}

Designing a portable DPV potentiostat requires first-principles analysis,
in-depth knowledge of electrochemistry, mixed-signal electronics and embedded
software, and engagement with the current literature
(Chapter~\ref{chap:literature}). Nanoampere currents must be resolved while a
Wi-Fi radio shares the supply, with the cell inside the feedback loops
(Section~\ref{sec:stability}). The requirements conflict in a public-health
problem with no regulatory framework (Section~\ref{sec:dpv_applications}),
there is no obvious solution (Table~\ref{tab:afe_approaches}), and no standard
prescribes how such an instrument should be validated
(Chapter~\ref{chap:testing}). The work crosses into analytical chemistry
(Section~\ref{sec:requirements}) and is a system problem spanning hardware,
firmware and two user interfaces (Chapters~\ref{chap:hardware_design}
and~\ref{chap:software_design}).

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- GA 1
\galabel{app:ga1}%
\ga{GA~1: Problem-solving}{Identify, formulate, research literature and
analyse complex engineering problems reaching substantiated conclusions using
first principles of mathematics, natural sciences and engineering sciences
with holistic considerations for sustainable development.}
&
The lack of a low-cost, standalone DPV instrument for antiretroviral residues
in South African water was identified, formulated as an aim, scope and
measurable requirements, and the literature was researched to locate the gap.
The design was analysed from first principles, from the decay of the cell
currents to the stability of the front-end, and unexpected results were
reasoned out from the data. Sustainability and social context were
considered, as these residues are not monitored in South Africa.

Mathematics was applied in the transfer functions and phase margin of the TIA

electrochemistry to explain how DPV rejects capacitive current; computing in
the firmware and averaging filter; and the instrumentation specialisation in
the amplifier, grounding and power design. Formal modelling appears in the
stability model, and engineering reasoning in tracing a common gain error to
the shared conversion chain.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- GA 3
\galabel{app:ga3}%
\ga{GA~3: Engineering design}{Design creative solutions for complex
engineering problems and design systems, components or processes to meet
identified needs.}
&
The OpenStat was designed as a complete system to meet the requirements,
comparing alternatives at each major decision. Its creative element is the
dual interface: an onboard display for field use and a browser dashboard. For
safety, no supply exceeds 5\,V, the Li-Po cell is protected and ferricyanide
is kept from acid and light. Components cost R\,2\,226, against R\,55\,400 for a
commercial instrument.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- GA 4
\galabel{app:ga4}%
\ga{GA~4: Investigations, experiments and data analysis}{Demonstrate
competence to conduct investigations of complex engineering problems using
research methods, including research-based knowledge, design of experiments,
analysis and interpretation of data, and synthesis of information to provide
valid conclusions.}
&
Antiretroviral residues are an emerging contaminant with no South African
regulatory limit, and the investigation evaluated whether low-cost DPV can
help address this. Five progressive tests each added one element of a real
measurement, so that each deviation could be traced. Critical thinking was
applied in judging agreement: correlation alone was not accepted, and the
instruments were compared against the PalmSens4's own repeatability. Peak
height proved to be electrode-limited, while the 24\,mV offset remains
unexplained.
&

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- GA 5
\galabel{app:ga5}%
\ga{GA~5: Use of engineering tools}{Demonstrate competence to create, select
and apply and recognise limitations of appropriate techniques, resources and
modern engineering and IT tools, including prediction and modelling, to
complex engineering problems.}
&
The tools created were the AD5941 driver (adapted from the Analog Devices
library) with automatic range selection, a firmware averaging window, Randles
dummy cells and the comparison scripts. Those selected and applied were KiCad,
JLCPCB, PlatformIO, Git, the IPC-2221 calculator, the laboratory instruments
and \LaTeX{}. Limitations were recognised: instrument accuracy bounded the
calibration to about $\pm1\,\%$, and the integrated front-end limits the
compliance voltage and current range.

The project's purpose is itself sustainable: low-cost monitoring of water
