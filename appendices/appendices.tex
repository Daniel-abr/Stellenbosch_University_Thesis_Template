\section{AD5941 Capacitor Selection}
\label{appen:hw_caps}
 
This appendix gives the full reasoning behind the capacitor values summarised
in Table~\ref{tab:hw_caps} of Section~\ref{sec:hw_caps}.
 
\section{Supply Decoupling: \SI{100}{\nano\farad} and \SI{10}{\micro\farad}}
\label{sec:hw_supply_decoupling}
 
Each supply pin has a local \SI{100}{\nano\farad} ceramic capacitor, and AVDD
has an additional \SI{10}{\micro\farad} bulk capacitor. The MT-101 tutorial
prescribes two tiers of capacitors: a large capacitor, typically
\SIrange{10}{100}{\micro\farad}, within about \SI{5}{\centi\metre} of the IC,
and small ceramics, typically \SIrange{0.01}{0.1}{\micro\farad}, as close to
the power pins as possible, optionally with a ferrite bead in series
\cite{adi_mt101}. Analog Devices applies the same principle in its precision
converter datasheets; for the AD7714, for example, a \SI{10}{\micro\farad}
capacitor is used in parallel with \SI{0.1}{\micro\farad} on each analog
supply \cite{ad7714_ds}.
 
\paragraph{Target impedance.}
The worst-case AVDD current step is the sum of all the subsystems of the
AD5941 switching on at once \cite[p.~14]{ad5941_ds}, which is approximately
\SI{10}{\milli\ampere}.
% CHECK: page 14 of Rev. G is the reset / digital I/O table. Confirm the page
% for the supply-current figures. Also, the ferrite bead section uses 10.1 mA
% -- use one value throughout.
The \SI{1.82}{\volt} reference has an AC PSRR of \SI{60}{\decibel}
\cite[p.~11]{ad5941_ds}, which corresponds to supply noise being attenuated by
a factor of about 1000. A \SI{10}{\milli\volt} supply disturbance therefore
appears as \SI{10}{\micro\volt} on the reference, which is a factor of 5.5
below $1~\text{LSB} = \SI{1.82}{\volt}/2^{15} = \SI{55.5}{\micro\volt}$
\cite[p.~7]{ad5941_ds}. Taking \SI{10}{\milli\volt} as a conservative limit on
the supply disturbance, the target impedance is
\begin{equation}
  Z_t = \frac{\Delta V}{\Delta I}
      = \frac{\SI{10}{\milli\volt}}{\SI{10}{\milli\ampere}}
      = \SI{1}{\ohm}.
  \label{eq:hw_ztarget}
\end{equation}
 
\paragraph{\SI{10}{\micro\farad}.}
The impedance of a capacitor falls as frequency rises, so a given capacitor
holds the rail below $Z_t$ only above a certain frequency. Setting the
capacitor impedance equal to $Z_t$ and solving for frequency gives that lower
limit:
\begin{equation}
  |Z_C| = \frac{1}{2\pi f C} = Z_t
  \quad\Rightarrow\quad
  f_\text{min} = \frac{1}{2\pi C Z_t}
               = \frac{1}{2\pi\,(\SI{10}{\micro\farad})(\SI{1}{\ohm})}
               = \SI{15.9}{\kilo\hertz}.
  \label{eq:hw_fmin}
\end{equation}
Above \SI{15.9}{\kilo\hertz} the capacitor can supply the sudden current
demands of the chip quickly enough that the AVDD voltage barely moves. Below
\SI{15.9}{\kilo\hertz} the \SI{3.3}{\volt} regulator holds the rail stable.
 
\paragraph{\SI{100}{\nano\farad}.}
Rapid switching inside the AD5941 draws short current bursts from the supply.
The \SI{10}{\micro\farad} capacitor cannot deliver bursts this fast. A small
capacitor placed directly at each supply pin has far less inductance in its
path and can deliver them, covering the frequencies from a few megahertz to
beyond \SI{100}{\mega\hertz}. A value of \SI{100}{\nano\farad} is recommended
for this role in standard decoupling guidance
\cite{adi_mt101, ott2009emc, horowitz2015art}, and the calculations below show
that it is adequate for this design.
 
Every capacitor resonates with its own equivalent series inductance (ESL) at
its self-resonant frequency, above which it behaves as an inductor. With a
typical ESL of about \SI{1}{\nano\henry} for the 0805 package used on this
board \hwtodo{reference for 0805 ESL}:
\begin{equation}
  f_\text{SRF} = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{L_\text{ESL}\,C}}:
  \qquad
  f_{\text{SRF},\,\SI{10}{\micro\farad}} = \SI{1.6}{\mega\hertz},
  \qquad
  f_{\text{SRF},\,\SI{100}{\nano\farad}} = \SI{15.9}{\mega\hertz}.
  \label{eq:hw_srf}
\end{equation}
The \SI{10}{\micro\farad} capacitor is most effective up to around
\SI{1.6}{\mega\hertz}, which makes it the right part for the slower range,
roughly \SI{16}{\kilo\hertz} to a few megahertz. The \SI{100}{\nano\farad}
capacitor keeps improving up to around \SI{15.9}{\mega\hertz}. Above that its
impedance is set by the ESL alone,
\begin{equation}
  |Z| \approx 2\pi f L_\text{ESL}
      = 2\pi\,(\SI{100}{\mega\hertz})(\SI{1}{\nano\henry})
      = \SI{0.63}{\ohm},
  \label{eq:hw_esl_100mhz}
\end{equation}
which is still below the \SI{1}{\ohm} target, so it covers the fast range
adequately.
 
\section{Internal Nodes: \SI{470}{\nano\farad} and \SI{4.7}{\micro\farad}}
\label{sec:hw_internal_nodes}
 
These capacitors connect to nodes inside the AD5941, specifically its voltage
references and internal regulators. Their correct values depend on internal
circuit details that Analog Devices does not publish, so they cannot be
derived independently and the datasheet values are used directly.
 
\paragraph{\SI{470}{\nano\farad}.}
The datasheet specifies \SI{470}{\nano\farad} for three pins, namely
\texttt{VREF\_2V5}, \texttt{VBIAS\_CAP} and \texttt{DVDD\_REG\_1V8}
\cite[pp.~11, 20--21]{ad5941_ds}, but does not explain how this value was
chosen. Capacitors on such nodes are normally sized to suit the stability and
noise requirements of the on-chip reference buffers and regulators
\cite{rinconmora2009ldo}, and those requirements cannot be assessed without
internal design data. The datasheet value was therefore adopted as specified.
 
The datasheet gives no value for \texttt{AVDD\_REG} \cite[p.~20]{ad5941_ds}.
The \SI{470}{\nano\farad} specified for the other internal regulator output,
\texttt{DVDD\_REG\_1V8}, was used as the closest available guidance.
 
\paragraph{\SI{4.7}{\micro\farad}.}
The datasheet pin description recommends \SI{4.7}{\micro\farad} on
\texttt{VREF\_1V82}, the \SI{1.82}{\volt} reference of the ADC
\cite[p.~19]{ad5941_ds}, again without stating the reasoning. Its
specification table, however, lists \SI{0.47}{\micro\farad} as the test
condition for the same pin \cite[p.~11]{ad5941_ds}, so the datasheet is not
internally consistent. The \SI{4.7}{\micro\farad} recommendation was followed
as the more conservative of the two. The ADC draws a small pulse of charge
from this reference during every conversion, and a larger capacitor holds more
charge, so the reference voltage dips less with each pulse.

\section{TIA Noise Gain with a Series Cell}
\label{app:tia}

At the inverting input of the TIA the cell is represented by the impedance
$Z_c = R_\Sigma + 1/sC_{dl}$ to the solution node, which the control loop
holds at a fixed potential, and the feedback network by
$Z_f = R_\mathrm{TIA}/(1 + sR_\mathrm{TIA}C_f)$. Applying Kirchhoff's current
law at the inverting node, with $V_n$ the node voltage and $I_\mathrm{in}$ the
faradaic current,
\begin{equation}
  I_\mathrm{in} = \frac{V_n}{Z_c} + \frac{V_n - V_\mathrm{out}}{Z_f},
  \qquad
  V_n = -\frac{V_\mathrm{out}}{A(s)}.
  \label{eq:app_kcl}
\end{equation}
Eliminating $V_n$ gives the closed-loop transimpedance
\begin{equation}
  Z_T(s) \equiv \frac{V_\mathrm{out}}{I_\mathrm{in}}
    = -\frac{Z_f}{1 + NG(s)/A(s)},
  \qquad
  NG(s) = 1 + \frac{Z_f}{Z_c},
  \label{eq:app_zt}
\end{equation}
so the loop gain is $A(s)/NG(s)$ \cite{graeme1996photodiode}. With $C_f = 0$,
\begin{equation}
  NG(s) = 1 + \frac{sR_\mathrm{TIA}C_{dl}}{1 + sR_\Sigma C_{dl}}
        = \frac{1 + s\left(R_\mathrm{TIA}+R_\Sigma\right)C_{dl}}{1 + sR_\Sigma C_{dl}},
\end{equation}
which is Equation~\eqref{eq:ng_series}. Setting $R_\Sigma = 0$ recovers the
photodiode case, $NG = 1 + sR_\mathrm{TIA}C_{dl}$, whose noise gain rises
without limit.

With $A(s) = 2\pi\,\mathrm{GBW}/s$, the phase of the loop gain is
\begin{equation}
  \angle L(jf) = \SI{-90}{\degree} - \arctan\frac{f}{f_z} + \arctan\frac{f}{f_p}.
\end{equation}
For $f_c \gg f_z$ the second term is close to \SI{-90}{\degree}, so
$\phi_\mathrm{PM} = \SI{180}{\degree} + \angle L(jf_c) \approx
\arctan(f_c/f_p)$, which is Equation~\eqref{eq:pm_approx}. Above $f_p$,
$|NG| \approx 1 + R_\mathrm{TIA}/R_\Sigma$ and $|A| = \mathrm{GBW}/f$, so the
crossover is at $f_c = \mathrm{GBW}\,R_\Sigma/(R_\mathrm{TIA}+R_\Sigma)$. A
margin of \SI{45}{\degree} requires $f_c \geq f_p$:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathrm{GBW}\,R_\Sigma}{R_\mathrm{TIA}+R_\Sigma} \geq
  \frac{1}{2\pi R_\Sigma C_{dl}}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  R_\Sigma^2 \geq \frac{R_\mathrm{TIA}+R_\Sigma}{2\pi\,\mathrm{GBW}\,C_{dl}}
  \approx \frac{R_\mathrm{TIA}}{2\pi\,\mathrm{GBW}\,C_{dl}},
\end{equation}
since $R_\Sigma \ll R_\mathrm{TIA}$, which gives Equation~\eqref{eq:rmin}.

With $C_f$ fitted, $Z_f$ falls above $1/(2\pi R_\mathrm{TIA}C_f)$, so the
noise gain falls from its plateau at \SI{-20}{\decibel} per decade and adds
phase lead at the crossover. Because the approximations above lose accuracy
when $f_c$ lies near $f_p$, the margins in Table~\ref{tab:pm} were computed by
solving $|A(jf)/NG(jf)| = 1$ numerically with the full expression for
$NG(s)$, including $C_f$.
